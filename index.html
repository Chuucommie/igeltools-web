02 / The icon language

Every icon starts here.

Two icons, one hand. The same hedgehog, the same engraving, a different field colour and prop for each app. Everything that comes next inherits the rules, so the family reads as a family.

Field
One solid colour per app. Blue #216DE7 for TVRemote, garden green for Notes. No gradient, no border, no gloss — the system adds the corner radius.
Line work
Cream paper and ink, drawn like an engraving. Hatching carries the texture instead of shading.
Character
The same hedgehog — Igel — glasses and all, drawn by the same hand in every icon.
Prop
One object says what the app does: a remote, a spiral notebook and a pencil.
Fill
The drawing runs to the edge of the tile. No frame, no lettering, no small print.
Next

TVRemote is blue, Notes is green. The reserved slot keeps the field, the character and the line work; only the colour and the prop change.

03 / How it is built

Small, local, honest.

The rules Igel TVRemote is held to, and the ones every app after it inherits.

01

Device to device

Commands go from your device straight to the TV over your own network. There is no cloud relay in the middle.

02

Nothing to sign up for

No account, no ads, no analytics. Pairing secrets live in the Keychain, and nothing is uploaded anywhere.

03

Wi-Fi, not infrared

No IR blaster and no line of sight to keep. The TV only has to be on the same network as your phone.

04

Built for real routers

SSDP, Bonjour and a subnet sweep run in parallel, and every protocol accepts a manual IP address when discovery is blocked.

05

A real keyboard

Type into a TV search box with the keyboard you already know, instead of arrowing through an on-screen grid.

06

Honest about limits

Fire TV is marked Limited and Apple TV is out of scope. The app says so instead of pretending otherwise.

04 / Supported TVs

Nine platforms, one remote.

Support is about protocols, not brands you install something for. If the TV is on your network and speaks one of these, the app can drive it.

Fire TV is marked Limited: current Fire OS no longer lets third-party apps control the TV locally, so it needs developer options and ADB debugging switched on. Apple TV and infrared-only TVs are not supported.
